## ⚠️ READ FIRST — the four things we never claim

Not optional, and not legal advice — these are standing constraints on OMERTÀ's public messaging.
Everything below is written to respect them. **New copy must too.**

| Never say | Why |
|---|---|
| **Any earnings / income / "make money playing" claim** | Paying players real-value assets at scale raises money-transmission, employment and securities questions that vary by jurisdiction. The mechanics ship; the *promises* wait for counsel to clear exact wording. Describe systems factually — never outcomes. |
| **Anything about token price going up** | Price-appreciation marketing is the fastest route to a securities problem. Describe what the token *does*, never what it will be *worth*. |
| **That anyone can cash out real stocks** | The in-game "Going Legit" book is a **status collectible at a fixed in-game price**. Real-asset delivery is a future phase gated on counsel, KYC and geofencing, and would **not** be available to US persons. Never blur these. |
| **That referrals pay a percentage of anyone's earnings** | It is a flat, one-time, cash-only finder's fee, capped at two levels. That distinction is the anti-pyramid line and it is load-bearing. Never "revenue share", never "downline". |

## 2. The wedge — why this is different

Four claims, each defensible, in the order they land hardest.

### 1. Death is real, and it is the best part
Most "permadeath" costs you an hour. OMERTÀ's death is a **legal event** — an estate runs in a single
transaction. Cash is looted by the killer. A business can change hands. Your street is gone forever.

What survives is deliberate: prestige, the dynasty's holdings, monuments, lifetime records,
marriages, a fraction of your honour, and a quarter of your standing with the city's fixers. Roughly
25 account-level things carry to the heir; roughly 35 character-level things burn.

And the death *creates content*. A **vendetta** is sworn between the two bloodlines. Repeat it and it
escalates — Vendetta → Blood Feud → War of Extinction — each lasting longer than the last. Settle it
with revenge, or end it at a sit-down if both sides agree. There is a public leaderboard of the
deadliest active feuds in the city.

### 2. The city is run by players, not by us
- **Families** hold turf, run wars, pool treasuries, buy institutions.
- **The Commission** is real player government: the top five families by *this season's* standing hold
  seats, vote weekly, and the majority decree binds the entire server for seven days. Close the
  safehouses. Ban new wars. Halve the price of lying low. The head seat can veto; the floor can
  override.
- **Diplomacy** — pacts that make war between two families impossible, and anti-hegemon **coalitions**
  that let smaller families fight whoever is dominant at a discount.
- **Markets are player-made.** Cars auctioned. Goods traded at docks. Loans written by players — and
  the paper on those loans is itself tradeable.

### 3. The Law is an antagonist, not a fail state
Heat fades. Underneath it a **federal case** builds silently. Cross the line and you're **indicted** —
then it's a courtroom: take a plea, buy a jury, or demand trial. Lose and the state seizes a share of
everything liquid. Staked assets are safe; the rest isn't.

Two escapes cost money: bribe the investigator, or keep a lawyer on retainer. Two more cost your
name: **flip** on a rival — drop your case, put the heat on them, and wear the permanent mark of a
rat, which strips your family's protection forever — or take **witness protection** and vanish.

And if they get you, there's somewhere to go. **The Pen** is a full location: work the yard, buy
contraband from a bent guard, pay the yard boss for protection, join a prison faction, shank a rival
who's inside with you, get thrown in the hole, smuggle a burner phone to order a hit on the outside,
or plan a co-op breakout and walk out a wanted fugitive with hunters on you.

### 4. The books balance — and that's a testable claim
The trust story. Lead with it for developer and crypto-native audiences.

Every unit of value is enumerated. Each currency has a **closed vocabulary** of reasons it may move
for. **Eighteen named checks** reconcile every pot in the game — bounty escrow, market escrow,
auction escrow, tournament escrow, the loan book, the casino's house book. Drift by more than a cent
and **the build fails and an alarm fires**.

We also wrote **seven harnesses** whose only job is to attack our own claims: an economy simulator
that plays through the public API and asserts the sweep; a progression harness that plays as a
plausible human and measures what they experience; a chaos harness that SIGKILLs the worker mid-sweep
to prove the resume pays exactly once; a load harness that reads Postgres's own deadlock counter; and
a liquidity harness that fills a town and asks whether anyone was on the other side of the trade.

**We have red-teamed our own game 58 times and published every report.**

## 3. The world

**The city.** Six districts, each with a mechanical reason to be there. The Docks (smuggling, wash
houses) · The Canal · The Foundry (crafting) · The Cathedral — the only district that starts free ·
The Brick · The Neon Mile (casino, fights, nightlife).

**The weather.** A daily event track shifts crime odds and prices. Regional supply shocks make one
district cheap and another rich — published a week ahead, so you can plan. A day/night clock means
the Bureau works business hours and convicts harder during patrol.

**The other guys.** Five NPC cartels hold reservoirs the whole server grinds down together. Beat one
badly and it *rises up* later — defends harder, stops paying tribute, and retakes its turf if you
left it undefended. Five of the six core districts start under cartel occupation; the only way to
free them is to fight.

**The residents.** The city is populated. NPC residents are real characters — they hold cash, list
prices, post loan offers, place buy orders, and can be robbed or killed like anyone. They exist so
the boards aren't empty on day one, and they're **labelled as residents** rather than passed off as
people.

**The fixers.** Six named NPCs whose standing you earn: Doc Moretti keeps you alive, Vinnie the Match
handles wet work, Bella Bang-Bang sells guns, Big Tuna moves freight, the Madame runs the floor,
Mickey the Corner trains fighters. Daily leads, weekly favours, multi-day errands — and grudges if
you kill someone they liked.

## 4. The feature tour — 44 interlocking systems

### The street
**29 jobs**, each with a real choice: **case it** (safer, quieter, pays less), do it, or **go loud**
(bigger score and materials — but it draws the Law and a harder sentence). **60 cars** to boost, tune,
race, melt or auction. A gym, a bank whose deposits take two hours to clear, and travel.

### The earners
- **The Kitchen** — buy makings, upgrade a lab, cook, cut it, deal. Work your regulars quietly or move
  weight fast and double the heat. Hire a crew that sells while you're offline — and pay their wages,
  or they stop.
- **Business Empire** — five tiers of front from laundromat to casino, each upgradeable, each an
  income stream *and* private laundering infrastructure with its own capacity.
- **Territory rackets** — one per district, owned by whoever holds the turf, in six flavours from a
  safe numbers game to a hot counterfeiting operation. Fortify. Assign a specialist. Run special ops.
  Raid a rival's.
- **Rackets & assets** — 30 assets and 18 rackets that pay forever once bought.
- **The pad, the nut, the upkeep** — every passive stream has a recurring bill. Neglect it and it goes
  **cold** and pays nothing. This is the answer to idle-game bloat: wealth needs tending.

### The scores
- **Crew heists** — twelve jobs from a corner store to the Federal Reserve. Every seat is a named role
  with its own stat. Case the job first. And any member can silently **rat** it — the job blows, the
  rat walks, and the feed only ever says *somebody talked*.
- **Smuggling convoys** — bulk goods over land on a real 30-minute clock. Hire guards. Insure the
  freight. Players ambush you — and a lane you run too often becomes *known*.
- **The Port** — the sea route. Buy boats, upgrade hulls and engines, run contraband from offshore,
  dodge the Coast Guard, warehouse the goods and time the market. Or turn pirate.

### The blood
- **The contract board** — put a price on a man's head, publicly or anonymously. Name a specific
  hitman with an exclusive window. Fund it from a family treasury.
- **The hunt** — place a search, wait for it to mature, then fire. Hospitalize or kill.
- **Assassin's reputation** — five ranks from Associate to The Undertaker; an account legend that
  survives your death and a season count that doesn't.
- **Defence** — a safehouse (wealth-scaled, daily-capped so nobody lives off-grid), a hired bodyguard
  who takes one lethal hit for you, pre-paid revive insurance.
- **The Dueling Ladder** — ranked ELO PvP with weapon styles that beat each other, divisions, a belt.

### The vices
- **The Gambling Den** — street craps, the numbers, blackjack, heads-up hold'em, **multi-way ring
  poker with real betting streets**, tournaments, a weekly fight to bet on (and, if you hold the Neon
  Mile, fix), and **The Track** — a daily card with posted odds where the runners can be other
  players' animals.
- **The Speakeasy** — open a club, climb a decor ladder, run a back-room table, build renown by being
  seen. Rivals can stand over it and force a sale.
- **Boxing** — sign fighters, train them, take exhibitions, win the belt, defend it, headline **main
  events** the whole city bets on.
- **Street Races** — the circuit, wagers, **pink slips**, nitrous, and a scheduled Grand Prix.
- **The Stable** — own greyhounds and racehorses, train them, **breed** them, run them in the city's
  daily card and the crowd-bet **Futurity**.

### The shadows
- **The Wire** — pay to know. Tap a rival's line, pull a deep dossier, trace who's listening to you,
  sweep your own lines, plant **disinformation** so your watchers read cooked numbers, or recruit a
  human informant who sees through it. A subscription pushes alerts when a marked man moves.
- **Secrets & blackmail** — dig real dirt derived from what a player has actually been doing, then
  demand a price for silence or expose them and put the heat on their file.
- **The Black Market** — car auctions with hidden reserves and anti-snipe, district-pinned goods
  trades, standing buy orders.
- **Loan sharking** — write predatory paper, take a car as collateral, brand defaulters as welshers,
  and sell the debt to a collector with more muscle than you.

### The family
Found one, take tribute, declare war, seize turf, buy a seal and a **charitable foundation** that
quietly softens every member's conviction odds, build strongholds, sign pacts, form coalitions, take
a seat at the Commission.

### The long game
Marriages between dynasties that bury feuds. Named soldiers with permadeath of their own. A skill
tree with capstones, actives and grandmasteries. An estate you furnish and staff. An auction house of
unique numbered trophies. A server-wide **Megaproject** the whole city funds together, which
permanently changes the skyline and puts the biggest contributors on a plaque forever. **36
leaderboards**, plus one aggregate **City Standing** answering *who is actually winning?*

### Standalone posts — rotate one a day

**Mechanics that sell themselves**
1. In OMERTÀ, any member of your heist crew can silently rat the job. The feed never names them. It just says: *somebody talked.*
2. Money in your pocket can be taken off your body. Money in the bank takes two hours to clear. Banking is an act with a window.
3. You can pay to read every anonymous funder on the contract out on your own head. Anonymity is a product. So is piercing it.
4. Plant disinformation and every wiretap on you reads cooked numbers. No warning, no tell. The counter is a human informant — you can't feed lies to someone actually in the room.

## 14. FAQ — including the hard ones

**Is it free?** Yes. Free to start in a browser, no wallet needed.

**Do I really lose my character?** Yes, permanently. Your account and bloodline survive and your heir
inherits a meaningful head start — but that character is gone.

**Is this a crypto game?** There's an optional on-chain layer. You can play the entire game without
touching it. It is currently **dormant** and gated on legal review and a third-party security audit
before it goes live.

**Can I earn money playing?** *(Answer exactly like this.)*
> We don't make earnings claims. There's an in-game token with a published, fixed schedule and a set
> of in-game uses, and the mechanics are all documented. Whether that's worth anything to you isn't
> something we'll promise either way — and any future ability to move value off-game is gated on
> legal review, identity verification, and jurisdiction.

**Are those real stocks in the game?** The "Going Legit" book is a **status collectible at a fixed
in-game price**. It is not a securities product, you cannot sell it, and it does not track a real
market. A future phase for real-asset delivery is designed but not shipped, and would be gated on
counsel, KYC and geofencing — and would not be available to US persons.

**Is it pay-to-win?** No. Real-money purchases grant cosmetics, access windows and consumables —
never cash, never power, never anything on the balance curve. A free player can top every leaderboard.

**What stops bots ruining it?** Agents are welcome, publicly badged, rate-limited harder, and excluded
from specific anti-Sybil reward paths. They have their own leaderboard. Better competing openly than
pretending to be human.

**What stops one whale owning everything?** Passive income has recurring bills. Safehouses are
wealth-scaled and daily-capped. A kill can take one of your businesses. And coalitions exist
specifically so smaller families can gang up on whoever's dominant.

**Is the game finished?** The systems are built and tested. It's in closed alpha, and the on-chain
layer is deliberately not live.

**What happens to my stuff if you shut down?** Everything is off-chain in our database today, and
we'd tell you honestly rather than pretend otherwise. That's exactly why we haven't switched on
anything implying more than that.

## 16. Messaging guardrails — the full list

### Never say
- "Earn", "income", "profit", "salary", "side hustle", "get paid to play", "play-to-earn"
- Anything about token price, value, appreciation — "early", "presale", "moon", "up only"
- "Investment", "returns", "yield" **as an offer to the reader** *(naming an in-game system is fine —
  "the family yield" is a game mechanic, not a promise to you)*
- "Own real stocks", "trade real equities", "cash out your portfolio"
- "Extractable on-chain at launch" ← **retired, see the correction at the top**
- "Passive income" as a pitch to the human *(fine to describe fronts producing in-game income)*
- Any suggestion referrals pay a share of earnings, or that there's a "downline"
- "Guaranteed", "risk-free", or "audited" without naming who audited what, when

### Say instead
| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| "Earn $OMR by playing" | "$OMR is distributed on a fixed, published schedule" |
| "The token will appreciate" | *(nothing — this has no safe version)* |
| "Extractable on-chain at launch" | "An on-chain layer is built and deliberately not live — gated on legal review and a third-party audit" |
| "Own real Apple stock" | "A death-proof status collectible at a fixed in-game price" |
| "Play to earn" | "Play for keeps" |
| "Passive income" | "Earners that pay while you're away — and have bills" |
| "Refer friends and earn" | "Bring people in and the city pays a one-time finder's fee" |
| "Fully audited" | "58 published red-team reports, each dated, each point-in-time" |

### Standing rules
1. **Describe mechanics, never outcomes.** "There is a schedule" is a fact. "You'll earn" is a promise.
2. **Say what isn't built.** The chain layer is dormant. Saying so is a trust asset.
3. **Date every audit claim.** Our own index says they're point-in-time. So should you.
4. **Never imply US persons get the real-asset phase.** They don't.
5. **When in doubt, cut it.** The game is genuinely good. It doesn't need the extra sentence.
